## HEAD

### The question

**Overt**: Can every operator turn, typed as speech acts by the xlate
interpretation, be joined with commits and agent replies on one timeline, so
that the development record can be read *as of* any moment — which rules were
in force, and how each came to be?

**Covert**: McCarthy's Elephant 2000 (1998) asks for programs whose statements
are speech acts (requests, promises, commitments) and that may refer to their
own past. The futon stack already stores the past (XTDB, git, the evidence
store) and, since 2026-09-22, types Joe's turns as acts. What is missing is the
join, and the queries that make the past usable. 象/象不忘 states the design
rule: messages cite the record they concern; the database's as-of view is the
program's memory.

**Build guide (local copy):** `storage/references/mccarthy-elephant-2000/` —
the paper as PDF, TeX (formulas as text), plain text and the latex2html pages,
with a README giving sources and checksums. The TeX file carries ~780 lines
of McCarthy's working notes after `\end{document}` that the PDF omits,
including "Parsing the Past" and a section on promises. His pseudo-Prolog
`hasreservation` is the "in force as of T" query below in miniature: a fact
holds if an act in the history created it and no later act cancelled it.

### Why now (Joe, 2026-09-26)

Two queries:
1. **In force as of T** — the constraint/proposal acts before T that a commit

   rule is absent; as of 09-24T17:00 it is present; as of 09-25T21:00 the
   followup half is withdrawn.
2. **Derivation of R** — from a commit back to the operator acts that led to
   it, through the agent turns between them.
