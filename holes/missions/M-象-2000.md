## IDENTIFY

### The question

**Overt**: Can every operator turn, typed as speech acts by the xlate
interpretation, be joined with commits and agent replies on one timeline, so
that the development record can be read *as of* any moment — which rules were
in force, and how each came to be?

**Covert**: McCarthy's Elephant 2000 asks for programs whose statements are
speech acts (requests, promises, commitments) and that may refer to their own
past. What would it take for the futon stack to *be* such a program — for its
design patterns, rules and commitments to be addressable at any past moment —
rather than merely to keep logs of one?

### Motivation: the paper fits both what we need and what we can now do

McCarthy's paper looks like a good match for current needs **and** current
abilities.

- **Abilities.** Two of Elephant 2000's prerequisites now exist in the stack.
  - *Speech acts are typed.* feed.html
    (https://zone.hyperreal.enterprises/wip/turns/feed.html) shows Joe's turns
    split into fragments, each with one of 19 intents (report-problem,
    constrain, propose, approve, disagree…) and the library patterns it cites
    or rejects. Since 2026-09-26 every buffer is captured by default and
    interpreted by the reserved seat 象.
  - *The past is stored.* XTDB keeps every state of the evidence store and
    answers as-of queries; git does the same for code.
- **Needs.** Joe (2026-09-26): rewind "to right before red tape started" and
  debug how it came to be; build "a robust record", perhaps eventually "a fully
  classical (non-LLM) mining picture"; attest patterns to grow the library;
  "a large connected component that we can use to feed the war machine".

In principle, then, we should be able to **time travel via design patterns**:
ask what the stack's working rules were at a moment, and walk back from a rule
to the acts that produced it. **We cannot do that right now.** The acts, the
commits and the agents' replies sit in three stores that nothing joins, and
there is no query for "in force as of T".

### The gap

Elephant 2000 was, to Joe's knowledge, never implemented; if it was, it was not
adapted to agentic coding. McCarthy's setting is one program talking to
people (the airline-reservation example). Ours is an operator and dozens of
agents whose acts are dictated, delegated, parked and re-delivered — and
sometimes injected under the operator's name by the harness (see the filter in
*Source material*). The adaptation is the mission.

### Theoretical anchoring

- McCarthy, *Elephant 2000* — local copy below. His pseudo-Prolog
  `hasreservation` is the "in force as of T" query in miniature: a fact holds
  if an act in the history created it and no later act cancelled it.
- 象/ patterns in `futon3/library/象/`: 象不忘 (messages cite the record they
  concern; the as-of view is the program's memory), 言即行 (the type rides on
  the envelope; the receiver refuses rather than guesses), 诺必践 (a promise is
  a record with a deadline and a beneficiary), 两种规格, 答必真且中的, 翻译契约.
- **Programs that refer to the future.** Clojure can also write programs that
  refer to results not yet computed (promises, futures, delays), which
  complicates the picture: McCarthy's history is a completed past, while an
  agent's promise ("I'll bell you the sha") or a parked job names a future
  that may never arrive. Carried forward as a tension, not settled here.

### Scope

**In**
- A timeline joining operator acts (from the xlate analyses), commits (git) and
  agent replies (evidence store), keyed by time and session.
- Two queries: *in force as of T*, and *derivation of R*.
- The acceptance case below.

**Out (deferred)**
- A language or runtime in McCarthy's sense; this mission builds the memory and
  its queries, not Elephant syntax.
- Future-referring acts beyond recording them as open (see anchoring).
- Replacing LLM interpretation with a classical miner; the mission keeps the
  data a classical miner would need.

## MAP

Survey, not design. Questions (Joe, 2026-09-26, Q1–Q2; Q3–Q4 carried forward):

- **Q1. Was Elephant 2000 ever implemented before the agent era?** If so, find
  the reference implementation. → `holes/labs/M-象-2000/MAP-Q1-prior-implementations.md`
- **Q2. XTDB against the paper.** Which of the paper's requirements do XTDB's
  temporal features meet, which does futon1b expose over HTTP today, and what
  is missing? → `holes/labs/M-象-2000/MAP-Q2-xtdb-vs-elephant.md`
- **Q3.** Where the view lives (futon1b query vs a futon3c script) — follows
  from Q2.
- **Q4.** How future-referring acts (promises, parked jobs, Clojure futures)
  enter a history McCarthy treats as closed.

Findings (2026-09-26):

- **Q1:** no pre-agent implementation of Elephant 2000 as designed; McCarthy
  "contemplates" the interpreter (§7). Partial: Babar (Clojure, 2013; speech
  acts + queryable commitment history), elephantKanren (miniKanren, 2015; the
  §7 history-matching runtime, no speech acts), ElGolog, Horton. See
  `MAP-Q1-prior-implementations.md`.
- **Q2:** McCarthy's `exists(t, commitment x)` (tex l.1395–1398: arose before
  t, not revoked since) is XTDB's valid-time write + valid-time delete + read
  at T. futon1b does this today for hyperedges only. The acceptance case needs
  valid time (when acts happened), read with current knowledge. See
  `MAP-Q2-xtdb-vs-elephant.md`.

| ready (no new code) | missing (the work) |
|---|---|
| XTDB 2.1 valid/system time, as-of reads | as-of params on `/evidence` (today: string compare on `:evidence/at`) |
| hyperedge upsert/retract with valid time, as-of GET (`futon1b_server.clj:128-141`, `:875-880`), tested | typed acts in the store: intents live only in `storage/operator-turns` files; every stored Joe turn is claim-type `:question`, harness notices included |
| 3,284+ mined turn analyses with intents, citations, rejections, candidates | origin tagging so harness/park turns are not Joe's acts |
| commits as evidence (`5146606d` absent at 09-24T16:00, present at 17:00) | a rule object that can arise and be revoked (acts → rule → commit links) |
| McCarthy's paper, local; Babar and elephantKanren as references | cancellation/withdrawal semantics; future-referring acts (Q4) |

**Joe, 2026-09-26 — reframing Q5 and the withdraw act:**

- **Q5 (revised).** Reconstructing how an incident like the red tape came
  about can be done from the Minard-era analysis (see M-the-perfect-crime and
  `futon2/holes/labs/wm-contract/E-cascade-real/rules-from-turns/`). The more
  interesting question is **prevention**: could lightweight inference over the
  design patterns themselves, run as acts arrive, flag such an incident before
  it happens? elephantKanren (Byrd; re-matches rules against the whole event
  history on each new event, ~60 lines of miniKanren) is the model to study —
  core.logic is its Clojure counterpart. MAP question: what would the 09-24
  history have looked like to such a rule set at 16:20, when the enforcement
  proposal arrived? That is, which patterns in the library, already on file,
  bear on "add an enforcement followup" (e.g. the inbox-zero family) and would
  have warned.
- **Withdraw (Q6).** No intent in the vocabulary means withdraw. Today a
  withdrawal lands under whichever label fits its surface form — a keyword scan
  of the mined fragments finds them spread over ~15 labels (direct,
  constrain, qualify, disagree, redirect, …). Chip: the ChipWits operator in
  the Workshop's OPERATORS box, row 2 column 4 (an x with a dotted arrow up to
  the right) is `op-drop-stack`
  (`~/chipwits-forth/linux/assets/ibol/op-drop-stack.png`), not used by the
  turnfeed intent–chip mapping. Proposed: a `withdraw` intent with that chip.

Q3 (where the view lives) leans to futon1b hyperedges: the one temporal
write/retract/read path that already works and is tested.

**Q5 reconstruction (2026-09-26, from the evidence store):**
`holes/labs/M-象-2000/MAP-Q5-1620-reconstruction.md`. The library as of 16:20
held `inbox-zero/gate-fails-loudly`, whose violation signature ("a consumer
that repeats steady red until it is ignored") is what the 42 notices became.
The live futon3a retrieval ranked it 3rd for Joe's 16:20 turn — six seconds
*after* claude-11 committed the rule — and its output only feeds a sigil in
the Emacs buffer. **Joe (2026-09-26): we cannot necessarily afford to run things before the
agent acts — which is why we need the ability to rewind.** So the design
target is not pre-act gating but *detect after, rewind cheaply*: the post-hoc
retrieval (already paid for) flags the act; the as-of record names the point
just before it; the rewind undoes what the act caused and lets the decision be
re-taken with the warning in view. Also: `turn-commits` attributes other seats' commits to
a turn; turn ids are offset by one between user and assistant records.
