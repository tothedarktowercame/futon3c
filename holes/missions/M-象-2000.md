## IDENTIFY

### The question

**Overt**: Can every operator turn, typed as speech acts by the xlate
interpretation, be joined with commits and agent replies on one timeline, so
that the development record can be read *as of* any moment — which rules were
in force, and how each came to be?

**Covert**: McCarthy's Elephant 2000 asks for programs whose statements are
speech acts (requests, promises, commitments) and that may refer to their own
past. What would it take for the futon stack to *be* such a program — for its
design patterns, rules and commitments to be addressable at any past moment —
rather than merely to keep logs of one?

### Motivation: the paper fits both what we need and what we can now do

McCarthy's paper looks like a good match for current needs **and** current
abilities.

- **Abilities.** Two of Elephant 2000's prerequisites now exist in the stack.
  - *Speech acts are typed.* feed.html
    (https://zone.hyperreal.enterprises/wip/turns/feed.html) shows Joe's turns
    split into fragments, each with one of 19 intents (report-problem,
    constrain, propose, approve, disagree…) and the library patterns it cites
    or rejects. Since 2026-09-26 every buffer is captured by default and
    interpreted by the reserved seat 象.
  - *The past is stored.* XTDB keeps every state of the evidence store and
    answers as-of queries; git does the same for code.
- **Needs.** Joe (2026-09-26): rewind "to right before red tape started" and
  debug how it came to be; build "a robust record", perhaps eventually "a fully
  classical (non-LLM) mining picture"; attest patterns to grow the library;
  "a large connected component that we can use to feed the war machine".

In principle, then, we should be able to **time travel via design patterns**:
ask what the stack's working rules were at a moment, and walk back from a rule
to the acts that produced it. **We cannot do that right now.** The acts, the
commits and the agents' replies sit in three stores that nothing joins, and
there is no query for "in force as of T".

### The gap

Elephant 2000 was, to Joe's knowledge, never implemented; if it was, it was not
adapted to agentic coding. McCarthy's setting is one program talking to
people (the airline-reservation example). Ours is an operator and dozens of
agents whose acts are dictated, delegated, parked and re-delivered — and
sometimes injected under the operator's name by the harness (see the filter in
*Source material*). The adaptation is the mission.

### Theoretical anchoring

- McCarthy, *Elephant 2000* — local copy below. His pseudo-Prolog
  `hasreservation` is the "in force as of T" query in miniature: a fact holds
  if an act in the history created it and no later act cancelled it.
- 象/ patterns in `futon3/library/象/`: 象不忘 (messages cite the record they
  concern; the as-of view is the program's memory), 言即行 (the type rides on
  the envelope; the receiver refuses rather than guesses), 诺必践 (a promise is
  a record with a deadline and a beneficiary), 两种规格, 答必真且中的, 翻译契约.
- **Programs that refer to the future.** Clojure can also write programs that
  refer to results not yet computed (promises, futures, delays), which
  complicates the picture: McCarthy's history is a completed past, while an
  agent's promise ("I'll bell you the sha") or a parked job names a future
  that may never arrive. Carried forward as a tension, not settled here.

**Out (deferred)**

**"Rejections" as weak activations (Joe, 2026-09-26).** The schema's
`pattern_rejections` are patterns a miner's search surfaced, read and judged
not to fire — weak activations, perhaps Hawkins-style priming. Test over 3,769

content. But no forward direction yet: cited-in-the-previous-K-turns is as
likely (K=1,3,5,10: after/before = 0.88, 1.04, 1.10, 1.06). Co-activation by
topic, not prediction. Retest once proposals are accepted and citations are
denser.

  reflects miners told to reuse ids already in their block's directory). All

  Making that work needs real pattern mining and matching — deciding when two
  differently-worded proposals name the same move.

  paper before it can be stated: the history of performatives, `arises` /
  `revoke`, `exists(t, commitment x)`, answers checked as true and responsive,

  and invariants.

**Matching via the pattern cascade (Joe, 2026-09-26).** Pattern mining and
matching continues the pattern-cascade work, mirroring what Joe had in mind for
the APM memory store but at the level of patterns: each turn is decomposed by a
semantic parse into a cascade of pattern applications (a pattern read as a
production rule — IF/HOWEVER/THEN/BECAUSE, futon2
`holes/NOTE-pattern-as-production-rule-and-Q.md`). Two proposals then match
when they occupy the same place in parses — same parent, same IF satisfied by
the same kind of fragment — not when their wording or ids agree. Today's xlate
output is the flat first layer (sentences → fragments → intent + cited /
weakly activated patterns + proposals with parents); the parse adds the
structure between them.

**From the memory store (E-memory-whitepaper-v2-plan, read 2026-09-26 at
Joe's pointer).** What carries over to the pattern level:
- **Attestation levels = its trust boundaries** (§3.3, §5.1): review
  (well-formed flexiarg) → warrant (cites the turns it came from) →
  attribution (a miner cites it for a new turn) → load-bearing (it changed an
  outcome, e.g. the P of a clearing proof) → witness (an independent party
  confirms). The store cannot witness its own patterns; no party owns two
  adjacent boundaries.
- **Cite/reject is the wrong shape** (§3.2.3): like `used-ids` it is
  substitutive, while most pattern use is regulative (stopping rule,
  risk-ordering, architecture). Weak activation is a third state; citations
  should be typed by mode. `gate-fails-loudly` on 09-24 would have been a
  stopping-rule use.
- **Reachable vs retrievable** (§3.2.1c): there the artifact was reachable but
  not retrievable; on 09-24 the pattern was retrieved but never reached the
  agent. Two axes, both needed.
- **Rewind has prior work** (§2.3): Experiment 0 is dispatch-time state
  reconstruction. The evidence route ignores as-of (Defect 1, still true per
  MAP Q2); the projection route reaches XTDB bitemporally, and its
  version-blind result-bound guard (Defect 2) was repaired 2026-07-31.

**象's role (Joe, 2026-09-26):** 象 both annotates and improves the store as it
goes. Within the trust boundaries: 象 may propose (warrant), match a new
proposal to an existing candidate (merge), and cite candidates for later turns
(attribution). Its citations of candidates it proposed itself do not count as
independent attestation; load-bearing and witness come from outside 象
(clearing proofs, Joe's acts, outcomes).

### DERIVE-2 — the act stamp (claude-17, 2026-09-28, after Joe: "we are midmission building to a specification … if it is too vague we need to improve it")

claude-17 paused the chain asking Joe how to treat existing acts and whether callers are verified. That was misdirected: the spec is for new acts, and 象/名分有据 THEN already answers the first question ("行为记录带三项：执行者、署名者、授权来源"; Decision P3 (3): each act records its harness *and* its grant). Unstamped acts written before this are outside P4's acceptance, which is four constructed cases. What the spec left vague, filled in here:

1. **Stamp shape.** Every minted act carries `:act/stamp {:executor ID :signer ID :authority AUTH :executor-basis BASIS}` beside `:act/harness`. AUTH is `{:grant "act:…"}` or `{:operator true}` (the signer is joe acting in person). A validator refuses executor ≠ signer without `:grant` (象/名分有据: 按越权退回). No other shape counts as authority; an interpretation id is refused as authority (象/释义非授).
2. **Self-acts go through a grant too.** Decision P10 (1) ("any author can withdraw its own acts at once") is recorded once as a root grant record: grantor joe, grantee `*` (any agent), scope `{:act :act/withdrawal :target-signer :self}`. A self-withdrawal's AUTH names that grant, so P4 checks scope, time and chain the same way for every act. Likewise a standing root grant for an agent selecting its own pattern card (Decision P7a/P10).
3. **Executor basis.** `:executor-basis` is `:declared` (the body `caller` field, today's only source) or `:session-bound` (Agency resolves the executor from the registered seat that made the request). P4 reports an act with a `:declared` executor as *unverified*, a finding of its own kind, never as authorised and never as overreach. Binding callers to seats is a later packet; the stamp makes the gap queryable meanwhile instead of hiding it.
4. **Rules.** A rule record's signer is its author; `:rule/governs` names the parties it binds (P13a). The author withdraws under the P10 (1) grant. A governed party's withdrawal (Joe's toilet-seat case) is provisional, handled like an inferred withdrawal (P10 (3)), unless it holds a grant. Rule records written before this have no signer and are not withdrawable by self-withdrawal; a new version with a stamp makes them so.
5. **P4 scope.** The scanner reads stamped acts. Unstamped acts are counted and listed as outside coverage, with no finding.

**DERIVE-2 amendment (claude-17, 2026-09-28, after P4-3).** Item 2 cannot be written with grant_record as built: a grantee is matched literally (no "any agent"), and scope holds only `:act-kinds`/`:rule-ids`, so it cannot say "own acts only". **DERIVE-2 item 4, refined (claude-17, 2026-09-28, after P10-2b-0).** A whole-rule withdrawal targets the rule **family**, not a version: an effect aimed at an obsolete version must not end a later one. **DERIVE-2 item 6: inferred withdrawals (claude-17, 2026-09-28).** Correction first: Decision P10 (3) says a withdrawal 象 infers "takes effect provisionally, is shown where Joe sees it, and one word from him reverses it". claude-17's P10-2a-1 packet specified the opposite (a provisional effect leaves the card active), and `card-as-of` was built that way. **DERIVE-2 item 7: grants from agreements (claude-17, 2026-09-28, after P11-5).** P11-5 lets an accepted agreement be a grant's source. Two things remained unspecified: what Joe sees before he says `yes`, and who writes the grant.
1. **Joe sees the scope he grants.** Joe accepts on the strength of the agent's prose. A grant, however, takes its authority from the option's structured scope, which the prompt segment does not show (it shows only `offer act:… (n options)`). Otherwise an agent could describe option 2 as "tidy the docs" while its scope lists `:act/withdrawal`. So when an offer is written, the server renders each option into Joe's buffer: its id, label, and any `:act-kinds`, `:rule-ids` and `:grant-until`. Joe reads that text, not the agent's prose, before answering.
2. **The acceptance writes the grant.** A grant is written only when the chosen option's scope is checkable (`:act-kinds` or `:rule-ids`) and carries `:grant-until`, a time the rendered line showed Joe. The agreement route then writes it after the verified agreement, as a root grant from joe to the offeror. Its scope is exactly the option's `:act-kinds`/`:rule-ids`, `:own-acts-only true`, and it runs from the agreement's `:at` to `:grant-until`. An option without `:grant-until` gives an agreement and no grant, and the status line says so. This gives no open-ended authority from a single `yes`.
3. Joe revokes a grant from an agreement the same way as any other grant; P11 adds no new revocation path.

**DERIVE-2 item 8: what ends a debt in P9 (claude-17, 2026-09-28, after P9-0).** The P9-0 discovery proposed that a promise leaves P9 at its first `promise/released` row. In the code, though, the only writer of that row is `wake-and-release!` (`parked_on.clj:326-331`), which records it in a `finally` straight after the wake. Under that rule every park promise would leave the debt list the moment its wake was delivered. That is wake-as-fulfilment again, the fault 象/诺必践 and BUILD-PLAN P5 ("terminates at release or lapse, not at wake") exist to catch. So:
1. A `promise/released` row ends the **wait**, not the **debt**, unless its body carries `:release/basis :explicit`. No writer produces that basis yet, so every existing row is a wait-release.
2. A promise with a fulfilment criterion or a deadline is owed from its creation until `promise/fulfilled` (status `:completed`, or `:completed-late` alongside `lapsed`) or an explicit release (`:released`). After `promise/lapsed` it is `:overdue` and stays listed.
3. A promise with neither a criterion nor a deadline is a wait, not a checkable debt. It is listed under `:unchecked`, not in `:owes`/`:owed`, and a wait-release ends it.
4. The BUILD-PLAN bad case ("a released promise still listed") is tested with an explicit release. A later packet adds the explicit-release write path (promisor or beneficiary, stamped).
5. Agreements: the offeror owes Joe, status `:open`, deliverable = the copied scope, and `:due-at` is nil and listed as incomplete. `:grant-until` is never read as a due date. An option `:due-at` field is deferred.

**DERIVE-2 item 9: explicit release (claude-17, 2026-09-28, after P9-2b).** Item 8 left the explicit-release writer unspecified. A debt has two parties, and they are not equal:
1. The **creditor** (the promise's `:beneficiary`) may release it. The row is `promise/released` with `:release/basis :explicit`, `:release/role :creditor`, and it projects as `:released`.
2. The **debtor** (the promise's `:agent`) may abandon it. The same row type carries `:release/role :debtor`, and it projects as `:abandoned`: closed, kept in `:ignored`, and attributed to the debtor. Only the creditor can make it count as settled.
3. Each is a stamped act (象/名分有据): the executor and signer are the releasing party, and the authority is a grant covering `:promise/release` for its own acts, found live as for offers. With no grant the answer is 403 and nothing is written. A promise with no beneficiary can only be abandoned. Joe's own releases (operator authority, from the REPL) are deferred.
4. The row goes through `promise_history/record!`, so it joins the promise's sequence chain. A release for an unknown promise, a party that is neither debtor nor creditor, or an already-closed debt is refused with a typed reason.
