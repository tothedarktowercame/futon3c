# Four more project languages: clients, collaborators, community, newcomers (draft, 2026-11-08)

DRAFT by a reader (subagent) for claude-6 and Joe to edit, 2026-11-08. It
follows the form of
`PROJECT-LANGUAGE-learning-and-patterns-2026-11-08.md` (languages L and P) and
the method of `forward-model/the-forward-model-is-a-pattern-cascade`: "holes
are patterns that should exist and do not, rather than as a list of missions."

Input: the four sections "clients & income" (54), "collaborators" (35),
"community & governance" (23) and "newcomers & users" (21) of
`~/notes/cascade-live-outline/speculative-benefit-lines-2026-11-08.md`. For
each of the 133 items I read the header and the motivation or IDENTIFY
section in the primary checkout (not the whole file). Every pattern id below
was checked to exist under `futon3/library/`, and its conclusion was read.
**HOLE** means no library pattern was found for the move; the search was by
directory listing and conclusion reading, so a hole may turn out to be a
pattern under another name.

Each crown says who is better off, following Joe's axiom that a benefit
finds a context that sustains it. The evidence for "better off" comes from
`benefit-reading-2026-11-08.md`. Placements are first placements, not
readings: an item placed here can still be wrong about its own benefit.

Two items were moved between lines because the keyword was wrong but the
benefit was real: E-futon-workshop-demo (keyword "clients", placed in N) and
E-memory-causal-integration (keyword "community", placed in K).

## Shared patterns and holes across all six languages

- **One hole sits under four crowns.** L10 (*a learner who is not the
  builder*), P12 (*the language is tried by people outside*), N1 and, in
  substance, G1 all say no one outside the builder has used the thing yet.
  K1 is the only crown with realised cases (Rob, JUXT). That makes Rob's
  use of mfuton and the XTDB report the nearest existing examples of what
  the other crowns ask for.
- **One hole is shared by C and N.** C5 and N4, *say "this is different"
  in the reader's terms*, are the same move made to a buyer and to a
  newcomer. M-joe-told-me-about-futon and E-consulting-positioning are its
  two items.
- **G3, *cui bono as an argument that can fail*, contains all six crowns.**
  Each crown is one claim about who benefits, and the "what has happened"
  paragraphs are the first evidence for or against each claim.
  M-formal-arxana names this as the missing layer of Cascade Live.
- **workshop/feedback-reaches-every-participant** appears in K7 and G8.
  Its `ways-of-working/` twin is one of M-ways-of-working's declared ways.
  It joins collaborators to governance: Rob's report that led to the
  M-weird-modernism fix is the one realised case.
- **nomad/self-certifying-artifact** appears in C8 and K6. The XTDB
  benchmark is the realised instance: a collaborator artefact that could
  also count as buyer evidence. The `nomad/*` patterns come from
  M-digital-nomad-patterns, an item in P2.
- **peeragogy/wrapper** appears in K10 and N6. **peeragogy/pattern-language**
  (L2/P2) and **creativity-commons/ARGUMENT** (L2, G2) connect learning,
  the Alexandrian line and governance. Peeragogy's patterns appear in four
  of the six languages.
- **C2 (stays-alive-and-paid) and G9/G10 (shared maintenance, succession)**
  are the two places where Joe's axiom, that benefit leads to viability,
  becomes something to check. C2 asks whether Joe's work is sustained.
  G9 and G10 ask whether the work would continue without him. In every
  language, Joe is today the only steward.
- **E-known-uses-and-competitors** (C5) also serves P12: its second use is
  "known uses" for the PLoP paper, which is the writers'-workshop test P12
  names.
- **Thin lines.** N rests on one mission and G on one engagement (UKRN). C
  has many items, but they are mostly about finding buyers; C1, C10 and C11
  have no open item that makes or prices an offer.
