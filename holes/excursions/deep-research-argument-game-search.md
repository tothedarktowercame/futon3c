# Engines already settle wins; learned argument play is missing

The literature already has the excursion's exact parts. What it does not have is the excursion's combination. Two-player argument games whose win is a computed property of a Dung framework have existed since 1995, and their winning strategies are proven sound and complete. LLM-proposer plus exact-engine systems such as AlphaGeometry, FunSearch and AlphaProof are the most successful designs of the last three years. Countdown and symbolic regression already cover most of the arithmetic s-expression game. What nobody has published is AlphaZero-style self-play in which agents *build* an argument framework until a computed semantics accepts their claim. Also unpublished: a formal argumentation semantics used as the RL reward for an LLM proposer, a natural-corpus versus random-target comparison on an arithmetic construction game, and any curve of how closely an active-inference habit tracks G over training. Most of the excursion's from-memory claims survive. Three need real correction. "Experts search less, not deeper" overstates de Groot. Experts do search more and deeper on hard positions. The 2016 habit prior accumulates the policy *posterior*, not a count of chosen policies. And Fountas et al.'s habitual network is a PUCT prior, not a pruner. Rob's caution is the best-supported claim in the file. It also moves: once an LLM proposes attack edges, the judged reward returns at the edge rather than at the terminal. On compute, no peer-reviewed CPU-only AlphaZero budget exists. Connect Four is the practical edge, at roughly a day of one desktop with a GPU. That makes Zone's 32 cores adequate for steps 1 and 2 and irrelevant to Lean.

## Masters recognise first, then search harder where it pays

Most of the chess-psychology memory is sound. The one correction changes how step 1 should be measured. Gobet & Simon's own five-second recall data show the canonical asymmetry. Masters recalled **92.0% of pieces in game positions against 32.2% for Class A players, but only 19.0% against 12.4% in random positions**. That is "nearly three times as many" in real positions and "only 1½ times as many" in random ones [V] ([Gobet & Simon 1998](http://www.chrest.info/Fribourg_Cours_Expertise/Articles-www/II%20Donnees%20empiriques/Gobet%20&%20Simon-1998-Memory.pdf)). The same paper states that "contrary to Chase and Simon (1973a)" a small skill effect on random positions "is observed consistently". So "hardly better" is the defensible wording. The chunk count also holds: Simon and Gilmartin estimated **10,000–100,000 chunks, with 50,000 the usual citation** [V] ([Gobet & Simon 1998](http://www.chrest.info/Fribourg_Cours_Expertise/Articles-www/II%20Donnees%20empiriques/Gobet%20&%20Simon-1998-Memory.pdf)). Gobet & Simon later proposed "as many as 300,000" from CHREST simulations. That is a model-derived figure, and sources disagree on which Gobet paper first gave it [S] ([Chassy & Gobet 2011, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0026692)). Template theory is Gobet & Simon 1996, implemented in CHREST. Templates are chunks with slots that hold "pointers to symbols representing plans, moves, strategical and tactical concepts" [V] ([Gobet & Simon 1998](http://www.chrest.info/Fribourg_Cours_Expertise/Articles-www/II%20Donnees%20empiriques/Gobet%20&%20Simon-1998-Memory.pdf)). Klein's recognition-primed decision model should be dated to the 1986 fireground study and *Sources of Power* (1998), where about **80% of commanders' decisions took under a minute** [S] ([Klein, Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/seeing-what-others-dont/202102/the-rpd-model-criticisms-and-confusions); [Fire Engineering](https://www.fireengineering.com/leadership/fireground-management/fireground-command-decisions/)).

The weak link is "experts search less, not deeper." de Groot found no gross differences in depth or in the number of moves considered. The 2011 think-aloud replication by Connors, Burns & Campitelli "support[s] de Groot's original conclusions". Yet in a combined analysis it reports a statistically significant difference in mean depth [S] ([Connors et al. 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2011.01196.x)). Campitelli & Gobet 2004 used very complex positions. Their title is "Skilled chessplayers search more and deeper", and the depth and node-count differences they found were sometimes about **10× larger than earlier studies** reported. Their account is that experts switch between recognition, on simple positions or under time pressure, and extensive search, on complex positions with time [S] ([summary in Chassy & Gobet 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0026692)). The excursion's "the measure is search saved" therefore survives, with a sharper reading. A good prior should cut nodes expanded on *familiar* positions, and it may *increase* useful search on unfamiliar ones. A single averaged nodes-to-solve figure would hide that split.

The AlphaZero parallel is confirmed and quantified. With no search at all, AlphaGo's RL policy network **won 85% of games against Pachi, a program running 100,000 simulations per move** [V] ([Silver et al. 2016](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring16/cos598F/Google-go-nature16.pdf)). AlphaGo Zero's raw network reached about **3,055 Elo against 5,185 for the full system**, so search added roughly 2,100 Elo on top of a strong prior [S] ([Fig. 6b as quoted](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/HAMsX36kCbbeju6M7/is-alphazero-any-good-without-the-tree-search/answer/pbZM2Wn4DFZkH9HXm)). The "thousand times fewer positions" figure comes in two versions. The 2017 preprint says 80,000 per second against 70 million for Stockfish [V] ([arXiv 1712.01815](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815)). The 2018 *Science* paper says 60,000 against 60 million [S] ([ChessBase](https://en.chessbase.com/post/the-full-alphazero-paper-is-published-at-long-last/1)). Expert Iteration had already made the dual-process framing explicit. Its network is "analogous to System 1" and tree search is "an analogue of System 2". A policy-guided MCTS beat vanilla MCTS **97% of the time, while doubling vanilla's iterations bought only 56%** [V] ([Anthony, Tian & Barber 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439)).

The machine-learning side supports "do not learn to execute." The excursion's account of Zaremba & Sutskever is accurate: "Deeper nesting and longer integers make the task more difficult." The detail it missed is that the *naive* curriculum "sometime[s] perform[s] worse than baseline". Only the combined curriculum won, and every reported accuracy uses teacher forcing [V] ([arXiv 1410.4615](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.4615)). The fixed-compute limit is a theorem of later work, not of that paper. Bounded-depth transformers cannot directly output answers to arithmetic expression tasks unless model size grows super-polynomially, but they can via chain-of-thought [V] ([Feng et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15408)). Fine-tuned GPT-3 is near-perfect in distribution and falls off sharply on deeper graphs. Its correct predictions track "linearized subgraph matching" rather than systematic computation [V] ([Dziri et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18654)). That is itself chunk recognition, in a form that fails silently. The specific formulation "name a subtree as a known chunk with a known value, hand the rest to the exact evaluator" has no source. In chess, recognised chunks "suggest good moves… further evaluated by limited look-ahead search" [V] ([Gobet & Simon 1998](http://www.chrest.info/Fribourg_Cours_Expertise/Articles-www/II%20Donnees%20empiriques/Gobet%20&%20Simon-1998-Memory.pdf)). Chunks propose; they do not settle values. The nearest computational antecedents are endgame tablebases inside alpha-beta and library learning (DreamCoder, Stitch [K]). The excursion should present it as its own synthesis.

## Active inference already amortises G, but nobody has published the amortisation curve

The three escape routes from enumerating policies are all real. Two of the attributions are wrong in detail, and the errors matter for the WM measurement. **Pruning** is exact as remembered. Sophisticated inference is a recursive Bellman-like search over belief states in which "searches over paths were terminated when the predictive probability fell below **1/16**". The same cutoff applies to the action prior. In the 8×8 maze, "only a tiny fraction" of the 10^10 (horizon 4) or 10^15 (horizon 6) paths were evaluated: "usually several hundred, which takes a few hundred milliseconds on a personal computer" [V] ([Friston et al., arXiv 2006.04120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04120)). The authors concede these are "rather trivial problems". The savings depend on precise beliefs, which an argument game against an unknown opponent will not provide. **Hierarchy** is confirmed in substance. The paper itself says that E "is usually conditioned upon hidden states at a higher level in deep (i.e., hierarchical) generative models" [V] ([arXiv 2006.04120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04120)). The deep temporal models article is **2017, *Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev.* 77:388–402**; the 2018 vol. 90 item is a corrigendum [V] ([PMC5461873](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5461873)). Pezzulo, Rigoli & Friston 2018 in *TiCS* was not re-fetched [K].

**The habit prior** needs a correction. Friston et al. 2016's prose says habits "accumulate evidence by simply counting the number of times they occur" and are "learned by observing one's own goal directed behaviour" [V] ([PMC5167251](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5167251/)). But the SPM reference code updates `e ← e + η·u(:,T)`, where `u` is the **policy posterior**, not an indicator of the chosen policy. Its posterior is exactly σ(ln E − F − γG) [V] ([spm_MDP_VB_X.m](https://github.com/spm/spm/blob/main/toolbox/DEM/spm_MDP_VB_X.m)). pymdp drops the F term and computes σ(ln E − γG) [V] ([pymdp control.py](https://github.com/infer-actively/pymdp/blob/master/pymdp/control.py)). Because the posterior already contains −γG, E drifts toward the time-averaged goal-directed distribution. That is the "mirrors what G would pick" mechanism. But E is a context-free tabular vector, so it does not generalise across states, and in tabular schemes it reweights every policy without pruning any. This bears directly on the excursion's description of futon2's `strategic_habit.clj` as "the 2016 counting habit." A counter of *selected* strategies is a hard-count variant. It is closer to Fountas et al.'s main-text rule ("trained to reproduce the last executed action") than to SPM's soft posterior update. The two converge only where the posterior is sharp.

**Fountas et al. 2020** is structurally the closest active-inference AlphaZero. The excursion's word "prunes" is wrong. The habitual network Q_φa enters the selection rule U = G̃ + c·Q_φa·1/(1+N) as a PUCT-style prior and samples actions at unexpanded nodes. It is trained by KL to the MCTS visit distribution, and that KL also sets the state precision ω. Planning stops when max P(a) − 1/|A| exceeds **T_dec = 0.8**, a separate threshold. Leaves are scored by explicit EFE terms over depth-3 rollouts, with **no learned leaf value**. Runtime was about **10 hours per agent with habitual or one-step strategies against about 4 days with full MCTS** [V] ([arXiv 2006.04176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04176)). A NeurIPS reviewer objected to the "habitual" label on the same grounds [V] ([review](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/865dfbde8a344b44095495f3591f7407-Review.html)). **Millidge** supplies the missing value. He fits G_φ to a TD target and states that the procedure "is analogous to bootstrapped value or Q function estimation procedures in reinforcement learning" [V] ([arXiv 1907.03876](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03876)). No paper combines the two into a full "AlphaZero for G". No paper explicitly maps AlphaZero's policy network onto E either. Grill et al.'s result is the formal bridge: the MCTS visit distribution tracks a reverse-KL-regularised policy optimisation toward π_θ, with weight shrinking as Õ(1/√N) [V] ([arXiv 2007.12509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12509)). Active inference's σ(ln E − γG) has the same form with a forward KL and a fixed weight. Reading "π_θ ≈ E" is a defensible synthesis, not a citation.

The excursion's claim about what is new holds. In every source the amortisation data is **the agent's own on-policy experience**. SPM adds its own posterior. Fountas trains on its planner's outputs and mentions only in passing that a "pre-recorded data-set" could be used. Millidge uses his own replay buffer. Çatal et al. use model-based imagination [V] ([arXiv 1904.08149](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.08149)). None uses multi-agent self-play or recorded behaviour of other agents, and none compares data sources. There is one subtlety. Training E on someone else's choices makes it an imitation prior, not an amortisation of one's own G. The nearest published agreement signal is Fountas's per-decision D_t = KL[Q_φa ‖ P_MCTS], and **no learning curve of it was found** [V] ([arXiv 2006.04176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04176)). The WM question — top-1 agreement of habit and G on recorded clicks, rising with experience — has no published precedent as a measured curve.

## Nobody has published the arithmetic hole-filling game or its baselines

The excursion specifies a partial tree with holes, filled leftmost-first from `+ - *` and small constants, with reward 1 if `eval` hits the target. **That game does not exist as a named benchmark.** Its two halves do exist, separately. Countdown supplies the exact-hit numeric reward, but its state is a multiset of remaining numbers, not a tree with holes. Stream of Search is the standard learned-search version: 4 inputs, targets 10–100, 10% of targets held out. A model trained only on optimal paths solves **25.73%**. Training on search streams raises that to **51.27% (seen targets) / 49.00% (unseen)**, and self-improvement with APA to **56.52% / 54.23%**. Average states explored in correct trajectories fall from **75.04 to 64.73**, a 12–14% cut [V] ([Gandhi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03683)). That is the closest published precedent for a "search saved" metric on an arithmetic target game. It compares learned against learned, not against plain MCTS. On Game of 24, Tree of Thoughts with GPT-4 reaches **74% against 4.0% for chain-of-thought and 7.3% for IO prompting** [K] ([Yao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601)). TinyZero's sub-$30 RL-on-Countdown result lives only in a README and a W&B log [BLOG] ([TinyZero](https://github.com/vra/TinyZero)).

Symbolic regression supplies the representation. Deep Symbolic Regression emits expressions token by token in pre-order — exactly "fill the next hole" — but its reward is a continuous data fit [K] ([Petersen et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04871)). SPL runs plain UCT over grammar productions applied to the leftmost nonterminal, with no learned prior [K] ([Sun et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13134)). DGSR-MCTS is the AlphaZero-shaped one: a pretrained transformer as PUCT prior, a learned critic, and reward 1 when R² ≥ 0.99. Its ablation is the strongest evidence for the excursion's "random positions defeat chunks". With a prior pretrained on random synthetic expressions, the solved fraction is **72.4% in-domain but 16.8% out of distribution without search, and 74.8% / 44.0% with MCTS** [V] ([Kamienny et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.11223v2)). The authors also replaced raw accuracy with a learned critic because raw accuracy "usually induces deceptive rewards". Two closer cousins come from theorem proving and program synthesis. HTPS built a purpose-made *Equations* rewriting environment and ran most of its ablations there; a soft critic was worth **+12.5 points** [V] ([Lample et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11491)). Write-Execute-Assess runs a policy and value over executed partial programs in a REPL, which is close to an s-expression evaluation game [K] ([Ellis et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04604)).

The baselines the excursion wants to measure therefore do not exist: solve rate and nodes expanded for learned value versus plain MCTS versus random, at increasing depth. **No published random/plain-MCTS/learned-prior table was found for Countdown, Game of 24, or any expression-construction game**, and **no study draws arithmetic targets or chunks from a real code corpus** [V, negative search] ([Gandhi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03683)). One property of the proposed design is worth stating. Free small constants, rather than Countdown's "use these given numbers", make the move space unbounded and the reward very sparse. HTPS and AlphaProof both handled sparse terminal rewards with generated curricula, not reward shaping [V] ([AlphaProof](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y.pdf)).

## Dung games with computed wins date to 1995; self-play over them does not exist

Step 2's game is not new. The argumentation literature has a family of Proponent/Opponent games in which "P wins" is a structural property of the dispute tree, with sound-and-complete winning strategies for each semantics. **The grounded game** is the canonical one. P opens with the query argument and players alternate attacks. P may not repeat an argument in a line and must strictly defeat. The argument is grounded-accepted iff P has a finite winning strategy [K] ([Modgil & Caminada 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-98197-0_6)). Credulous and sceptical preferred games come from Vreeswijk & Prakken 2000 [K] ([JELIA 2000](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-40006-0_17)). TPI disputes have proven efficiency properties, including unavoidable worst-case exponential length [K] ([Dunne & Bench-Capon 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(03)00076-6)). Caminada's grounded discussion game is polynomial in length [K]. Prakken's persuasion protocols generalise all of these to multi-reply dialogues with commitment stores, where the "current winner" is the grounded status of the dialectical graph [K] ([Prakken 2006](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0269888906000865)). Pease et al. 2017 belongs here, with one caveat. Its dialogue game "defines ordered operations on a shared information state represented in the Argument Interchange Format". TOAST and DungOMatic then compute the grounded extension as "the currently accepted, collaboratively constructed, proof" [V] ([Corneli et al., arXiv 1803.06500](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.06500)). The status is computed, but the game is collaborative, with **no winner and no terminal rule**. Its moves are Lakatos's methods of proof and refutation. That inventory was reconstructed from secondary sources because the CC BY primary PDF returned 403 [S] ([Pease et al., AIJ 246](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/lakatos-style-collaborative-mathematics-through-dialectical-struc/)). "AIF²" appears nowhere in the literature. It is a futon-local coinage over AIF (2006) and AIF+ (2008) [V] ([Reed et al. 2008](https://www.arg.tech/people/chris/publications/2008/comma2008-aif+.pdf)).

The reward is as cheap as Rob would want. **Grounded membership is in P**. Credulous preferred and stable acceptance are NP-complete, and sceptical preferred is Π2P-complete. In practice, the ICCMA 2023 virtual best solver, dominated by the SAT-based µ-toksia and Crustabri, solved **287–315 of 329 benchmark frameworks** within 1,200 s CPU, depending on the task, on frameworks far larger than any game would produce [V] ([Järvisalo, Lehtonen & Niskanen, ICCMA 2023](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/mjarvisa/papers/jln.aij25.pdf)). The Dynamic track's IPAFAIR interface supports incremental add/remove-then-requery. It is exactly the API a search engine needs, and Crustabri won all three Dynamic subtracks [V] ([ICCMA 2023](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/mjarvisa/papers/jln.aij25.pdf)). One trap is documented: a stable extension existed in only **206 of 329** frameworks. A stable-semantics reward can therefore go degenerate [V] ([ICCMA 2023](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/mjarvisa/papers/jln.aij25.pdf)). Learned evaluation is not needed for the reward. In the approximate track, the GNN entrant AFGCN ranked 4th–5th, solving **228 DS-PR instances against 300 for harper++**, which simply answers with the grounded extension [V] ([ICCMA 2023, Table 4](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/mjarvisa/papers/jln.aij25.pdf)). GNNs do predict acceptance "almost perfectly" in-distribution [V] ([Craandijk & Bex 2020](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/231)), which makes them candidates for a value prior, not for the judge.

Search over argument games exists, but in other configurations. MCTS selects persuasion moves over a *given* graph with a model of the persuadee [V] ([Hadoux, Hunter & Polberg](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11870)). MCTS solves "argumentative puzzles" [V] ([Riveret et al., ARGMAS 2014](https://www.mit.edu/~irahwan/argmas/argmas14/w12-07.pdf)). Yonas is a DRL+MCTS *acceptance solver* in which "the trained model guides the tree search at runtime" [V] ([ICCMA 2019](https://iccma2019.dmi.unipg.it/papers/ICCMA19_paper_8.pdf)). **No work was found in which agents add attacks or supports from a menu, by self-play, until a computed semantics accepts their claim.** The researcher flags this as absence after a limited search, not proof. The game-theoretic strand adds a warning for menu design. Under grounded semantics, truthful revelation is incentive-compatible only under restrictive conditions [K] ([Rahwan & Larson 2008](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/1402298.1402308)). A menu game will therefore exhibit strategic withholding, and the engine designer has to decide whether that is a feature.

## Search on a judge optimises the judge, so the judge stays out of the terminal

Rob's caution is the most robustly supported claim in the excursion. Under optimisation pressure, best-of-n and RL against a proxy reward model **raise proxy reward monotonically while gold reward rises and then falls**. This follows smooth laws in √KL, and a KL penalty acts only as early stopping [K] ([Gao, Schulman & Hilton 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)). Beam search against a process reward model degrades at high budgets by exploiting its errors [K] ([Snell et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)). Even near-exact verifiers have a ceiling. With imperfect unit tests, resampling gives unbounded gains only with a perfect verifier, and optimal sample counts are often below 10 [V] ([Stroebl, Kapoor & Narayanan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501v1)). LLM judges can be gamed outright: tokens such as ":" or "Thought process:" elicit false-positive rewards. In one RLVR run with a 72B judge, the policy collapsed to fewer than 30 tokens [V] ([Zhao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08794)). RLHF has taught models to raise human evaluators' false-positive rates [K] ([Wen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12822)).

The argument-specific evidence comes from AI-safety debate. Debate with more persuasive LLMs raised judge accuracy to **88% for human judges and 76% for LLM judges, against naive baselines of 60% and 48%**. But in *consultancy* — a single optimised arguer with no adversary — **judge accuracy fell as persuasiveness rose** [V] ([Khan et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.06782)). Debate's protection is real but modest and task-dependent: it gives "small or no advantage" over direct QA outside information-asymmetric tasks [V] ([Kenton et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04622)). The theorem-proving literature has drawn the practical conclusion. Every serious prover uses Lean, Metamath or HOL4 as the final reward and uses learned judges only for guidance [V] ([AlphaProof](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y.pdf); [HTPS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11491)). DeepSeek-R1 avoided neural reward models explicitly because of reward hacking at scale [K] ([arXiv 2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)). The ToT/RAP/LATS family looks like a counterexample, but it shows only that a judged *heuristic* can guide search when an exact terminal check exists [K] ([Yao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601)).

The literature adds two refinements to Rob's version. First, the operative property is the verifier's false-positive rate, not "learned versus computed": a complete rules engine for a finite game has a false-positive rate of zero. Second, a computed semantics moves the attack surface rather than removing it. Once an LLM proposes attack edges, the question "does this attack really hold?" is judged again, and degenerate topologies (odd cycles, self-attack, stable non-existence) become exploitable. ArgLLMs is the nearest neuro-symbolic design. The LLM generates pro and con arguments, and a gradual semantics computes the verdict, explainably and contestably. It does not search or train against that verdict [K] ([Freedman et al., AAAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02079)). **No paper was found that uses a formal argumentation semantics as the RL reward for LLM debaters.**

## No peer-reviewed CPU-only AlphaZero budget exists; Connect Four marks the edge

The cheapest published AlphaZero results all use a GPU, and none of them is peer-reviewed. OpenSpiel's alpha_zero documentation gives **no results, timings or hardware figures**. Its Python implementation runs on CPU, and the docs disclaim scaling "to superhuman performance in Go or Chess" [V] ([OpenSpiel docs](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel/blob/master/docs/alpha_zero.md)). The cheapest credible record is **AlphaZero.jl on Connect Four**: 15 iterations of 5,000 self-play games at 600 simulations per move, each iteration taking "between one and two hours" on an **i5-9600K (6 cores) plus one RTX 2070**. That is about 15–30 hours to near-solver play [BLOG] ([AlphaZero.jl tutorial](https://jonathan-laurent.github.io/AlphaZero.jl/stable/tutorial/connect_four/)). alpha-zero-general reached 6×6 Othello in about **3 days on one Tesla K80** [BLOG] ([alpha-zero-general](https://github.com/suragnair/alpha-zero-general)). A student project reports tic-tac-toe mastered after about **3,000 games** and Connect Four "decent" after about **100,000** [BLOG] ([CogitoNTNU](https://github.com/CogitoNTNU/AlphaZero)). Oracle's engineering series cut a Connect Four run from an estimated **450 to about 21 GPU-hours** by tuning [BLOG] ([Oracle part 6](https://medium.com/oracledevs/lessons-from-alpha-zero-part-6-hyperparameter-tuning-b1cfcbe4ca9a)). A 2022 paper cites **21–77 GPU-hours** for Connect Four [S] ([arXiv 2204.13307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.13307)).

On CPU only, the researcher made a back-of-envelope estimate for the AlphaZero.jl run: about 1.6 × 10⁹ network evaluations. At 1,000–5,000 small-ResNet evaluations per core-second, that is roughly **100–400 core-hours**, or about 3–14 hours of Zone's 32 cores if parallelism were perfect. This estimate is not published, and it puts Connect Four at the edge of CPU viability. 8×8 Othello and 9×9 Go sit another 10–100× beyond. Three levers cut simulation count. Gumbel AlphaZero learns 9×9 Go with as few as **two simulations** [K] ([Danihelka et al. 2022](https://openreview.net/forum?id=bERaNdoegnO)). MiniZero found that progressively raising simulation counts beats a fixed count [V] ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11305)). A September 2026 preprint reports that plain approximate value iteration in self-play learns **more accurate values than AlphaZero** on Connect Four and 7×7 Hex "at substantially lower training and inference cost"; its timings were not verified [V abstract] ([Boige et al., arXiv 2609.09094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09094)). The pre-LLM provers rlCoP and TacticToe ran MCTS with gradient-boosted or k-NN learners on CPU [K]. rlCoP solved over 40% more problems than its baseline at matched inferences [V abstract] ([Kaliszyk et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07563)).

Lean is confirmed as the wrong substrate for Zone. HTPS gave each Lean prover **48 CPU cores** and moved its ablations to Equations and Metamath "since Lean experiments are CPU intensive" [V] ([HTPS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11491)). DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5 ran Lean checks on "thousands of CPU cores" with 300 s sandboxes [V] ([arXiv 2408.08152](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08152)). A warm REPL pool checks a whole proof in about **0.75–1 s** [S] ([AXLE, arXiv 2606.26442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26442)). AlphaProof's main RL loop took **~80,000 TPU-days**, and its authors call that scale "likely beyond the reach of most academic research groups" [V] ([AlphaProof](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y.pdf)).

## What this does to the three cheap first steps

| Step | Status after the survey | What to reuse | What stays open |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Arithmetic s-expression game | **Not redundant; cheaper to design; one stopping rule needs changing** | Countdown/SoS as an external reference point; HTPS critic and policy-target lessons; LevinTS as a policy-only baseline; DGSR's OOD ablation as the template | Learned vs plain-MCTS vs random nodes-to-solve curve; natural-corpus vs random targets |
| 2. Pure Dung game | **The game is redundant (use the grounded game or Prakken's protocol); the learning is not** | Grounded game rules; IPAFAIR incremental solvers; min-backup over opponent replies | Self-play construction of an AF to a computed semantics |
| 3. Hybrid LLM proposer | **Cheapest to prototype by analogy, and the most hazardous** | AlphaGeometry/FunSearch split; engine enumerates legal moves | Formal semantics as RL reward; certifying LLM-proposed edges |

For **step 1**, the literature makes the build cheaper and sharpens the measure. The HTPS ablations give concrete defaults. Use soft, search-backed critic targets: hard 0/1 targets did *worse than no critic* on Equations, 63.1 against 65.6. Train the policy on minimal solutions, not raw visit counts: visit-count targets reached **37.5% on Equations against 78.1%** for minimal proofs [V] ([HTPS §7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11491)). The excursion's stopping rule — "if the learned value does not beat plain search, stop" — needs a stronger baseline than plain UCT. In DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5, **UCT without an exploration bonus scored 58.2% against 58.4% for plain sampling** [V] ([arXiv 2408.08152](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08152)). Policy-only best-first search reached 72.95% on miniF2F with no value network at all [S] ([BFS-Prover](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03438v2)). The fair comparison is learned value versus a policy-only search such as LevinTS, whose node expansions are bounded by depth divided by the policy's probability of the solution [V] ([Orseau et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10928)). That makes "search saved" a quantity with theory behind it. The excursion's change to a natural distribution is the most clearly unprecedented part of step 1, and DGSR's 72.4% → 16.8% collapse predicts it will matter.

For **step 2**, inventing the game would duplicate thirty years of work. The grounded game gives a polynomial, exact, binary reward. Stable semantics should be avoided because of the degenerate-reward trap. The open question is whether a learned policy has anything to add on a small, fixed menu, since a grounded check is cheap enough that exhaustive search may simply solve small instances. The learning only becomes interesting where move selection, not evaluation, is hard: large menus, private arguments, strategic withholding. The prover literature supplies the right search shape. AlphaProof's AND nodes use 1−Q to pick "the unproven subgoal perceived as most difficult" and back up the minimum [V] ([AlphaProof](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y.pdf)). HTPS warns that vanilla MCTS ignores "unlikely but critical moves from the opponent" [V] ([HTPS App. A.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11491)). An argument game maps directly onto both.

For **step 3**, the architecture is validated by analogy and hazardous in its specifics. AlphaGeometry solved **25/30 IMO geometry problems against 14/30 for its symbolic engine alone** [K] ([Trinh et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06747-5)). An LLM policy prior inside MCTS reached grandmaster-level chess [V] ([Schultz et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12119v1)). Kaggle's Game Arena shows why the engine must enumerate legal moves: **78% of Gemini 2.5 Flash's chess losses were illegal moves**, not strategic blunders [S] ([ChessArena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24239)). The hazard is Rob's caution, moved to the edge. For mathematical lemmas, an edge-level verifier (a proof checker) restores exactness. For argument content, it does not, and a pure Dung core with LLM-proposed edges inherits the consultancy failure mode at every edge it accepts.

## Conclusion

The survey reverses the excursion's implied order of novelty. The excursion treats the engine as the hard part and the learned search as the known part. The literature has the opposite. Computed-win argument engines, cheap exact solvers and the proposer-verifier architecture are mature. Learned self-play *over argument construction* has no precedent, and neither does the empirical question underneath step 1: whether a pattern prior from a natural distribution saves measurable search. The cleanest experiment the excursion could run is therefore not one of its three steps. It is DGSR's ablation transplanted to arithmetic s-expressions: corpus-drawn versus random targets, with nodes expanded under LevinTS as the measure. That isolates the chunk hypothesis with no argumentation machinery and no language model.

The active-inference correction matters more than it looks. SPM accumulates the policy *posterior*, while futon2 counts *selections*. So the WM's habit is not the 2016 habit, and a habit-versus-G agreement curve is unpublished. Running that measurement on recorded clicks would be both the cheapest test in the file and the only one that could put the excursion on the critical path, since it bears directly on the first critical-path trigger, a policy search the AIF work cannot do by enumeration. The [K]-tagged bibliographic details should be spot-checked before the excursion's paragraphs are rewritten: the argument-game proof procedures, ToT and AlphaGeometry figures, and Gao's overoptimisation forms. So should the Pease et al. move inventory, which needs the CC BY primary PDF.
